## Executive summary

Heading into 2026–2027, all four sectors are in a **synchronized, AI-capex-driven
upcycle**, with **memory/HBM the most extreme and most clearly supply-constrained
leg**. The chain is: hyperscaler AI capex (~$600–700B in 2026) → accelerators
(NVIDIA + custom ASICs) → HBM/DRAM (the binding constraint) → optical interconnect
(the *next* bottleneck). The clearest bounded-upside signal is internal to the
bull case: **SK Hynix itself flags that HBM prices "could enter a correction phase
after 2026"** as all three suppliers finish capacity expansions and HBM4
competition intensifies in H2 2026. That makes **late-2026 capacity arrival + the
HBM4 ramp the pivotal dates to watch.**

---

## 1. The setup: a trillion-dollar, AI-led semi market

- **Total semiconductor revenue crosses $1.29T in 2026 (+52.8% YoY from $842.8B),
  per IDC** (Apr 29, 2026), driven primarily by AI infrastructure. Gartner
  corroborates directionally (>$1.3T, +64%). *(confidence: high)*
  - **Caveat:** WSTS sees only ~$975B (+25%); 2025 baselines differ by ~$70B
    across firms. The $1.29T/+52.8% is an aggressive IDC-specific view, not
    consensus.
- This is **not broad-based** — it is concentrated in memory and AI accelerators.
  The rest of the semi complex (analog, autos, industrial) is along for a far
  milder ride.

## 2. Memory / HBM — the supercycle and its trajectory

**The most extreme leg, and the most clearly supply-locked.**

- **IDC: total memory revenue $226B (2025) → $594.7B (2026) → $790.4B (2027).**
  2026 DRAM $418.6B (+177%), NAND $174.1B (+138.5%). *(high)*
- **BofA (via SK Hynix): 2026 DRAM revenue +51%, NAND +45%; ASPs +33%/+26%; HBM
  market $54.6B (+58%).** *(high — but sell-side; BofA's Top Pick is SK Hynix,
  and its $54.6B HBM figure is far above other firms' ~$4–9B due to scope.)*
- **Near-term realized pricing is already spiking:** Samsung & SK Hynix reportedly
  pitched **Q1-2026 server DRAM prices up 60–70% QoQ** to Microsoft and Google;
  TrendForce later revised conventional DRAM 1Q26 even higher (90–95% QoQ). *(high)*
- **Why it's structurally locked:** HBM capacity is **pre-committed through 2026
  with allocations into 2027**, and **no meaningful new supply arrives until late
  2026 at the earliest.** 2026 memory capex favors HBM/process upgrades (hybrid
  bonding, taller stacks) over wafer capacity, so it barely lifts bit supply —
  IDC pegs 2026 DRAM bit growth at only ~16%. HBM cannibalizes commodity DRAM
  (~4× the wafer area per bit), tightening the whole DRAM market. *(high)*
- **How long it runs / the bound:** **SK Hynix's own outlook says HBM prices
  "could enter a correction phase after 2026"** as all three suppliers complete
  expansions (late 2026) and HBM4 competition intensifies (H2 2026). One secondary
  source (Blocks & Files) argues the supercycle runs *through 2028*; the verified
  signal is that the **risk window opens after 2026**, magnitude/timing unknown. *(high)*

**Runway vs. priced-in:** The near-term (through 2026) supply lock is about as
well-evidenced as a forward thesis gets. The **2027 leg is the contested one** —
it's where the bull (supercycle-through-2028) and the bear (post-expansion
correction) collide.

## 3. AI compute capex — the engine

- **Hyperscaler 2026 capex ~$600–700B** (+36% to +70% YoY depending on baseline
  definition), **~75% (~$450B) AI-directed.** IDC i4: +70% to ~$600B; ARK: up to
  $700B; Gartner: data-center systems >$650B (later revised toward $788B).
  Post-earnings tracking (CNBC, Feb 2026) runs even higher at $700–725B. *(high)*
  - The +36% vs +70% gap is **baseline-definition divergence, not contradiction.**
- **NVIDIA remains dominant:** calendar/TTM revenue ~8× from $27B (2022) to $216B
  (2025), consensus **+62% to ~$350B in 2026.** *(high)*
  - **Key nuance:** those are calendar/TTM figures. NVIDIA's FY2025 GAAP was
    $130.5B — don't compare the wrong basis.
- **Custom silicon erodes (not displaces) the lead:** ARK projects **custom ASICs
  >⅓ of the compute market by 2030** (total AI infra spend $500B 2025 → ~$1.5T
  2030). SemiAnalysis: Google could cut cost/computation ~62% with its own TPU
  internally; AMD now competitive on **small-model inference** TCO with a
  rack-scale solution (Helios/MI400) due **2H 2026**, while NVIDIA keeps the
  large-model/frontier-training lead. *(high — ARK is known-bullish.)*
- **Inference vs training:** the shift toward inference economics is what opens
  the door for ASICs and AMD; frontier *training* still favors NVIDIA's rack-scale
  systems.

## 4. Silicon photonics / optical interconnect — "the next bottleneck"

- **Optical transceiver demand exceeds supply ~2×**, driven by **Indium Phosphide
  (InP) laser constraints** (2025 InP wafer shipments 600–700k vs demand 1.5–2M;
  800G transceiver output 40–60% below demand through 2027). *(high; "factor of
  two" is slightly imprecise but bracketed by corroborating data.)*
- **NVIDIA committed $4B ($2B each) to Coherent and Lumentum** (Mar 2, 2026) to
  advance optical interconnect and **lock in capacity** — bundled with multi-
  billion-dollar purchase commitments beyond the equity. *(high; SEC 8-Ks.)*
- **Copper is hitting its limit.** NVLink domains scale from 72 GPUs (Blackwell
  NVL72) toward larger Rubin configs (NVL144, 576-GPU cross-rack); NVLink 6 at
  400G SerDes limits copper to <1m. This pushes toward an optical transition. *(medium)*
  - **Important reality-check:** the "mandatory / imminent" framing **overstates
    consensus.** NVIDIA and Broadcom say copper persists "as long as possible";
    TrendForce projects scale-up co-packaged-optics (CPO) penetration stays
    **low-single-digit through 2027**; **scale-out, not scale-up, is the first
    optical target.** Scale-up optical is largely deferred to Rubin Ultra/Feynman
    (2027+). Direction is sound; timing is the risk.
- **Under-discussed beneficiary — rack-scale connectivity:** **Astera Labs (ALAB)**
  posted record Q1-FY2026 revenue **$308.4M (+93% YoY)** on PCIe 6, guided Q2 to
  **$355–365M (+15–18% QoQ)**, and flagged **new design wins in custom + optical**.
  A direct line into the interconnect thesis. *(high; SEC 8-K.)*
- **The volatility is real:** on 2026-05-18, AOI −10%, Lumentum −9%, Coherent −6%
  in a single session on AI-optics profit-taking. This sub-sector trades sharply.
